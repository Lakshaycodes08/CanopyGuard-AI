\title{A Spatially Blocked Test of a Map-Class Canopy-Height Growth Prior in Northern Sonoma County}
\author{CanopyGuard-AI contributors}
\date{}

\begin{abstract}
Forecasting canopy-height change from repeat airborne light detection and
ranging (LiDAR) requires a baseline evaluated without using the target survey
as a predictor. We compare no height change, a map-class median increment,
and a Chapman--Richards curve on screened 2013--2022 observations in northern
Sonoma County. Five-fold spatial cross-validation with 2 km blocks finds
that the type-specific or pooled broad-group median reduces mean absolute
error (MAE) relative to no change by 0.121 m on forest cells selected
using later fire and Hansen loss records. In a post hoc analysis of all
eligible forest cells, the improvement is 0.062 m and its 2 km block
interval includes zero. The height-dependent curve adds no measurable
improvement. In a stage-two residual test pre-specified and committed
locally before fitting, without an independent timestamp, ridge and
LightGBM both increase MAE on the screened population and fail
the gate. A post hoc diagnostic shows that squared-error training can
itself worsen MAE relative to the median prior. These are preliminary
growth-prior results, not a validated
corridor clearance forecast. The LiDAR-loss-on subset is retrospectively
selected; persistent-forest selection still needs visual audit.
Conductor geometry is unavailable, so future span rankings would be
research prioritization rather than clearance or safety determinations.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Vegetation management near transmission lines motivates forecasts of canopy
change, but this study has not measured conductor clearance or produced
validated span rankings. The immediate question is narrower: does a simple
vegetation-map-class increment improve a 2013-to-2022 height forecast over
assuming no change, and does a height-dependent growth curve improve on that
increment? The study uses 2013 airborne LiDAR and vegetation mapping at the
forecast cutoff and reserves 2022 LiDAR for the outcome. Its present
contribution is a spatially blocked comparison of growth priors with
explicit treatment of label contamination and retrospective screening.

\section{Related Work}
Growth-aware corridor analysis predates this study. Mongus et al.\ used
repeat airborne LiDAR to predict growth and detect encroachment
\cite{mongus2021}; Chen et al.\ combined UAV LiDAR, species information,
and Richards curves for future tree encroachment \cite{chen2022}. These
studies motivate a simple baseline before fitting a more flexible curve
to map classes that are not species-pure.

Satellite height mapping and maintenance allocation address related tasks.
Lang et al.\ mapped canopy height from Sentinel-2 and GEDI with independent
airborne-LiDAR evaluation \cite{lang2023}. Pauls et al.\ released
retrospective annual ECHOSAT maps while stating that year-to-year height
changes lack direct validation against independent repeated observations
\cite{pauls2026}. Jumbo and Moghaddass connected image-derived vegetation
risk to crew routing \cite{jumbo2022}; Jaramillo-Leon and Leite studied
preventive trimming schedules \cite{jaramillo2022}. These works establish
that growth curves, satellite height mapping, and scheduling are not
new components. The present result concerns the measured value of a
map-class growth prior under a fixed spatial evaluation.

\section{Study Area and Data}
The configured northern Sonoma County box spans $122.90^\circ$--$122.74^\circ$
W and $38.475^\circ$--$38.82^\circ$ N. The California Energy Commission
map contains 41 published overhead transmission features intersecting it.
These are approximate line features, not conductor geometry \cite{cecsource}.
The analysis uses a stable 10 m cell grid in EPSG:6339, with 2013 and 2022
airborne-LiDAR canopy summaries and 2013 Sonoma vegetation-map classes
\cite{sonomaveg}.

Per cell, the label archive holds mean, 95th-percentile (p95), and
maximum canopy height at both epochs. The outcome is the p95 height
difference. The 2022 survey supplies the held-out outcome only. GEDI is
reserved for an independent cross-check; ECHOSAT is a product benchmark.
GEDI, ECHOSAT, and Sentinel-2 all postdate the 2013 cutoff and are excluded
from predictors in this experiment. Fire-perimeter and Hansen loss records
support retrospective sample screens, not model features
\cite{frapsource,mtbssource,hansensource}.

\section{Methods}
\subsection{Label construction and wire-quality control}
Canopy-height summaries are formed on matching cell identifiers after
within-epoch surface and terrain processing. The overlap rule retains
the highest minimum-coverage observation, then the higher total coverage,
then the lower tile identifier. It yields 1,478,103 unique cells; 198
disputed overlaps are excluded. Terrain from different epochs is not
directly subtracted as a height-change label. The 2013 p95 value may be
a predictor; no 2022 height enters a predictor.

Mapped transmission features may pass near unclassified wires, poles,
or towers. The wire-quality check found an excess of isolated tall
returns near published lines, especially in 2013, but point inspections
did not prove each return's cause. The p95 analysis excludes flagged
wire-suspect cells and cells within 10 m of a mapped line. This is a
documented quality choice, not a verified point-level classifier.

\subsection{Screens and priors}
The forest comparison excludes disputed overlaps, invalid height changes,
low starting canopy, unknown vegetation, mapped fire or Hansen loss, cells
near published lines, and wire-affected p95 cells. The forest
LiDAR-loss-off screen retains 125,888 cells. A second forest screen
removes cells with target-epoch LiDAR loss, leaving 76,980. Because this
uses target-epoch information, it is a retrospectively selected
undisturbed-canopy upper bound. Even the loss-off screen uses retrospective
fire and Hansen data; neither proves persistent forest. A random visual
audit of persistent forest has not yet been done.

For each training fold, the stage-one prior is the median observed p95
increment for the cell's 2013 Sonoma map class. Classes with fewer than
500 training cells use their broad forest group. The no-change baseline
predicts the 2013 p95 height. The Chapman--Richards curve is a sensitivity
comparison only. Its height parameter $A$ is the maximum of a cited
typical-upper height and the training-fold 99.5th percentile of paired
p95 heights. This rule was chosen \emph{after} literature-only ceilings
showed exceedances. It is an empirical map-class cutoff, not a biological
asymptote. The 2022 heights of a held-out fold do not set that fold's $A$.

\subsection{Spatial evaluation}
Square 2 km spatial blocks are assigned to five dispersed folds. Every
prediction is made for a block held out from its fit. MAE is computed
across held-out cells. Paired MAE differences use 2,000 whole-block
bootstrap draws with seed 42; a 95\% interval wholly below zero is the
comparison criterion. G3 evaluated both forest screens. The later
stage-two residual gate was pre-specified locally before fitting, without
an independent timestamp, with loss-off as its headline screen.

A post hoc population sensitivity retains all forest cells passing label
quality, 2013 canopy, vegetation, line-distance, and wire exclusions,
including cells later mapped as burned or lost. It refits the median
prior in the same five spatial folds at 1, 2, and 5 km block sizes.
This analysis tests how much the retrospective fire and Hansen screens
change the estimated gain; it was designed after the G3 result.

\subsection{Stage-two residual comparison}
The pre-specified target is observed p95 height change minus the
training-fold stage-one median increment. E4 ridge (regularization
parameter 1) and E5 LightGBM (maximum depth 4, 15 leaves, 400 trees,
learning rate 0.03, minimum 200 cells per leaf, feature fraction 0.6)
use 2013 LiDAR mean, p95, and maximum height; 2013 Sonoma map class;
and static elevation, slope, heat load, and 450 m topographic position.
The static terrain source is a 2012 USGS 3DEP 1/3 arc-second DEM
\cite{usgsdem}. Terrain is resampled to the existing 10 m label cells;
heat load follows McCune and Keon \cite{mccune2002}. All preprocessing
is fitted on each spatial training fold. Neither target-epoch LiDAR nor
post-2013 satellite observations enter the predictors. Both models
use the same five-fold 2 km blocks and 2,000-draw paired whole-block
intervals as the stage-one comparison. The loss-off screen is the
pre-specified decision screen; 1 km and 5 km blocks, all classes, and
the LiDAR-loss-on screen are declared sensitivities.

Ridge and LightGBM minimize squared-error objectives, whereas the
decision metric is MAE. A post hoc diagnostic therefore compares the
median prior with two models carrying no new predictors: one adds the
training-fold mean residual as a global intercept, and the other
replaces each training-group median increment with its mean. These
comparisons quantify an objective mismatch, not added feature skill.

On the retrospectively screened loss-off forest population, median
minus no-change MAE was $-0.121$ m
(95\% whole-block interval $[-0.145,-0.097]$). On the retrospectively
selected loss-on screen it was $-0.268$ m ($[-0.312,-0.227]$).
The type-specific or pooled median increment beats assuming no change
on both screens. The larger loss-on gain is an upper-bound result for
a selected subset.

The post hoc all-forest population has 402,728 eligible cells before
retrospective disturbance screening; fire and Hansen rules remove
276,840 of them for the loss-off comparison. At 2 km, the all-forest
median-minus-no-change MAE difference is $-0.062$ m
($[-0.144,+0.012]$), versus $-0.121$ m
($[-0.145,-0.097]$) after screening. The all-forest interval includes
zero at 5 km ($-0.016$ m, $[-0.079,+0.052]$), while its 1 km interval
is below zero ($-0.061$ m, $[-0.122,-0.002]$). The screened loss-off
gain persists at 1 km ($-0.121$ m, $[-0.140,-0.103]$) and 5 km
($-0.118$ m, $[-0.143,-0.093]$). These extra analyses are post hoc
and do not change the recorded G3 gate.

Curve minus median MAE was $+0.000$ m ($[-0.005,+0.005]$) with LiDAR
loss off and $+0.002$ m ($[-0.007,+0.013]$) with it on. The
height-dependent Chapman--Richards curve adds nothing measurable
under spatial cross-validation. With literature-only $A$, the curve
was worse than the median on the loss-on screen: $+0.012$ m
($[+0.001,+0.025]$). Eleven of 18 map-class fits touched a $k$
or $c$ bound, so their shape parameters are not interpreted biologically.

\subsection{Stage-two residual models}
On the forest LiDAR-loss-off 2 km screen (125,888 cells), the matched
stage-one MAE is 1.256 m. Ridge MAE is 1.336 m, a difference of
$+0.080$ m (95\% whole-block interval $[+0.060,+0.101]$). LightGBM
MAE is 1.309 m, a difference of $+0.052$ m ($[+0.029,+0.083]$).
Both fail the pre-specified rule, which requires the entire interval
below zero. No further settings were tried. On the retrospectively
selected loss-on subset, ridge and LightGBM improve over stage one by
$0.020$ and $0.031$ m respectively, but this upper-bound sensitivity
does not reverse the loss-off decision. The loss-off result remains
unfavorable at 1 km and 5 km blocks and across all admissible map
classes.

On the screened loss-off population, the post hoc intercept-only
squared-error model increases MAE over the median prior by $+0.028$ m
($[+0.015,+0.041]$); substituting type means increases it by
$+0.044$ m ($[+0.027,+0.067]$). Thus an objective mismatch can account
for part of the $+0.052$ m LightGBM and $+0.080$ m ridge deficits.
It does not establish how much of either fitted model's deficit is
caused by the objective rather than disturbance or other errors.

\section{Discussion}
The measured contribution is a map-class median increment that improves
on no change in the retrospectively screened forest population. The
post hoc all-forest comparison does not show a reliable gain at the
2 km or 5 km block scale. The curve sensitivity does not improve on
the screened median, and its post hoc $A$ values are not species
maxima. Sonoma map
polygons may contain mixed species; whether this or LiDAR outliers
explains tall empirical cutoffs is unverified. The 27 m
\emph{Acer macrophyllum} value is a champion-tree exception to the
otherwise typical-upper rule. The Jepson height entry for
\emph{Eucalyptus camaldulensis} still needs human verification.

The persistent-forest visual audit is incomplete. The LiDAR-loss-on
population is retrospectively selected and cannot represent an
operational forecast-time filter. ECHOSAT bands 1--7 map to
2018--2024, as confirmed by the corresponding author (J. Pauls,
personal communication, Oct. 2026). The maps were predicted jointly
using imagery through 2024, so ECHOSAT remains a retrospective
benchmark and supplies no 2013 predictor.
Whole-block bootstrap intervals hold fitted fold models fixed and
omit refit variance, so they may be too narrow. No conductor
geometry is available. Eventual span rankings would be research
prioritization, not clearance or safety determinations.

The stage-two loss-off failure shows that these fixed residual models
do not improve the median prior on this screened population. Better
loss-on scores may reflect retrospective exclusion of loss cells;
they cannot establish operational forecast skill. The present result
does not justify changing the pre-specified feature set or settings
after inspection. Because the models minimize squared error while the
gate uses MAE, their failure cannot be attributed solely to
unpredictable disturbance.
The stage-two analysis plan appears before the fit in local Git
history, but its commits were first pushed together after the fit;
there is no independent server timestamp for the plan before fitting.

\section{Conclusion}
A type-specific or pooled broad-group median height increment improves
the retrospectively screened 2013--2022 p95 comparison over no change.
The post hoc all-forest gain is smaller and uncertain at 2 km and 5 km.
Height-dependent Chapman--Richards curves add no measurable benefit.
Independent scientific
review of the post hoc curve rule and validation of persistent-forest
selection remain open before broader claims are made.
The pre-specified ridge and LightGBM residual models fail their
loss-off improvement gate. These comparisons do not support replacing
the median prior with either fitted residual model.
